继上次冲出中美两国之后，这次我又冲出亚洲和北美两洲了，三月第一周去了里斯本、巴塞罗那和巴黎。浪游记快的第七季就分三篇讲一下在这三座城市都干了什么。

不过里斯本是这三座城市里唯一让我有点失望的，所以虽然按照时间顺序它是第一个，不过先跳过去，从巴塞罗那开始写。

After making it out of both China and the US last time, this time I finally made it beyond Asia and North America too. In the first week of March, I went to Lisbon, Barcelona, and Paris. Season 7 of my travel notes will be split into three posts, one for each city.

That said, Lisbon was the only one of the three that left me a little disappointed. So even though it was technically the first stop, I'm going to skip ahead for now and start with Barcelona.

<div style="display: flex;">
  <div style="flex: 1; padding-right: 10px;">

	<p>来巴塞罗那的重点当然是看高迪的建筑，巴特罗之家的外立面真是绝美。之前讲到曲面的建筑我第一反应都是哈扎，像是广州大剧院或是望京soho，她的曲面是尺度巨大的冷白色。而百年之前高迪的曲面是来源于自然的温润暖黄色，从每个房间的墙面、天花板到细节装饰的门框、踢脚线，没有一处是横平竖直的，可能只有地面是平的吧，身处其中会感觉整个空间都在流动。</p>

	<p>Of course, the main reason to come to Barcelona is Gaudí's architecture. The façade of Casa Batlló is absolutely stunning. When I used to think about curved architecture, my first instinct was always Zaha Hadid — things like the Guangzhou Opera House or Wangjing SOHO. Her curves are massive in scale, cold, and almost pure white.

	But Gaudí, more than a century earlier, worked with curves inspired by nature — warm, soft, almost honey-colored. From the walls and ceilings of every room to the smallest details like door frames and baseboards, nothing is straight or flat. Maybe only the floor is level. Being inside, you really feel like the entire space is in motion.</p>

	<p>当年房东住二楼，上面的几层都对外出租。沿着楼梯向上爬，中间天井的瓷砖颜色从浅蓝逐渐过渡到深蓝，阳光透过走廊上的玻璃，波光粼粼的感觉，仿佛置身地中海的海底。</p>

	<p>Back then, the owner lived on the second floor, while the upper floors were rented out. As you walk up the stairs, the tiles in the central light well gradually shift from light blue to deep blue. Sunlight filters through the glass along the corridors, creating a shimmering, rippling effect — it feels like you're standing at the bottom of the Mediterranean Sea.</p>

米拉之家也很惊艳，最让我印象深刻的是爬到顶层的阁楼，无数道薄砖砌出高迪经典的拱形结构，像是鲸鱼的骨骼一样，又仿佛是圣家堂的预演。

Casa Milà was just as stunning. What impressed me the most was the attic at the very top — countless thin bricks forming Gaudí’s signature arches. It felt like the skeleton of a whale, or almost like a preview of the Sagrada Família.

  <p>Climbing further up the spiral staircase to the rooftop, the stair towers covered in white mosaic rise straight into the sky, and the chimneys stand like armored warriors.

It's a pity that during our week in Europe, all three cities were cloudy, so the lighting in the photos didn't turn out great.</p>

  <p>当然，这些都只是开胃菜，重头戏还是圣家堂。

来之前我在网上走马观花看了好多关于圣家堂的介绍，反而被绕得头昏脑胀的。其实圣家堂的结构并没有解说里的那么复杂，俯瞰的结构依旧是教堂惯用的十字架形。十字架短的一横两端分别开门，一端的外立面是雕刻着耶稣降生故事的诞生立面，另一端是讲述耶稣殉难故事的受难立面。而十字架竖条的底端是教堂的大正门，外立面就是还在建设中的荣耀立面。</p>

  <p>Of course, all of that was just the appetizer — the real highlight is the Sagrada Família.

Before coming, I'd skimmed through a lot of online explanations about it, and honestly just ended up more confused. In reality, its structure isn't as complicated as people make it sound. From above, it still follows the classic cross-shaped layout of a church.

At the two ends of the shorter arm of the cross are two entrances: one side is the Nativity façade, which depicts the story of Jesus's birth, and the other is the Passion façade, telling the story of his suffering and crucifixion.
At the base of the longer vertical axis is the main entrance, where the Glory façade — still under construction — stands.</p>

高迪的建筑都坐落在十九世纪末建成的扩展区，镶嵌在一个个方格街区里，林荫大道边奢侈品店云集。而我们住在哥特区，这里的街道狭窄曲折，像迷宫一样铺开，很符合我对中世纪的想象。巷子里散落着咖啡馆、餐厅和vintage店，我买到一件超喜欢的carhartt外套，开心（笑死从美国去欧洲结果又买美国牌子）。

酒店隔壁就是巴塞罗那主教座堂，散步时还遇到了另一处圣母教堂。半夜因为时差醒来睡不着，教堂的钟声每十五分钟就响一次。

audí's buildings are all located in the Eixample district, which was developed in the late 19th century — neatly arranged into grid-like blocks, lined with tree-shaded boulevards and luxury boutiques.

  We, on the other hand, stayed in the Gothic Quarter. The streets there are narrow and winding, spreading out like a maze — exactly how I imagine the Middle Ages.
  Cafés, restaurants, and vintage shops are scattered throughout the alleys. I even found a Carhartt jacket that I absolutely love in a vintage shop (kind of hilarious — going all the way from the US to Europe just to buy an American brand).

  Right next to our hotel was the Barcelona Cathedral, and while wandering around we also came across another church dedicated to the Virgin Mary.
  One night, I woke up in the middle of the night because of jet lag and couldn't fall back asleep — the church bells rang every fifteen minutes.

  <p>毕加索博物馆就藏身在哥特区暗无天日的小巷子里，从外面看毫不起眼，走进去也算小有洞天，低矮的楼房围合出一个比篮球场稍小的庭院，光线也因此明亮了点。毕加索青年时代曾在巴塞罗那学画，所以这里大多是他早期的作品，更全的馆藏要等到巴黎再看。</p>

  <p>The Picasso Museum is tucked away in the dark, narrow alleys of the Gothic Quarter. From the outside, it looks completely unremarkable, but once you step inside, it opens up into a hidden little world. Low buildings enclose a courtyard slightly smaller than a basketball court, letting in just enough light to brighten the space.

Picasso studied painting in Barcelona during his youth, so the collection here mainly features his early works. For a more complete collection, I'll have to wait until Paris.</p>

哦对了还去看了弗拉明戈舞表演。不知道为什么我订成vip票了，就坐在舞台下面第一排，近距离看着舞者们把舞台跺得震天响哈哈。而且vip票里自带的红酒意外地好喝，把我喝得更融入到那种热烈的氛围里了。演出结束之后已经是深夜了，头有点蒙蒙地走在忽明忽暗的小巷子里，路过一家还开着的纪念品店，花一欧激情购入了一个弗拉明戈舞冰箱贴。第二天清醒过来再看，感觉好丑哈哈。


Oh, and we also went to see a flamenco performance.
For some reason, I ended up booking a VIP ticket, so we were seated right in the front row, basically at the edge of the stage. I could watch the dancers up close as they stomped so hard the whole floor felt like it was shaking — it was amazing.

The VIP ticket also came with a glass of red wine, which was surprisingly good. It definitely helped me sink deeper into that intense, passionate atmosphere.

By the time the show ended, it was already late at night. Slightly tipsy, we wandered through the dimly lit alleys and passed by a souvenir shop that was still open. In a moment of excitement, I bought a flamenco-themed fridge magnet for one euro.

The next day, when I looked at it again sober… it felt kind of ugly. 😂

	<p>在巴塞罗那的最后一个下午去了桂尔公园，这本来是当年的豪华房地产项目，坐落在城市外围的山上，同样也是高迪设计的。跟之前去的几个地方相比，高迪在这里使用马赛克拼贴的技法已经臻于化境了。感觉就是在建筑工地上随便找了些同色系的瓷砖，打碎重新拼贴出来，虽然不费吹灰之力，但不知道为什么就赏心悦目。可惜我当时光忙着欣赏了，照片一张没拍，只打卡了一张公园入口台阶上的标志性的大蜥蜴。</p>

	<p>On my last afternoon in Barcelona, we went to Park Güell. It was originally planned as a luxury housing project, built on a hill on the outskirts of the city, and of course also designed by Gaudí.

	Compared to the other places I visited, his use of mosaic here feels almost perfected. It's like he just grabbed some tiles of similar colors from a construction site, smashed them, and casually pieced them back together. It looks effortless, but somehow it's incredibly pleasing to the eye.

	Unfortunately, I was too busy enjoying everything and didn't take any photos — except for the iconic mosaic lizard at the entrance.</p>

以上。周一傍晚到的巴塞罗那，周四下午飞去巴黎。此行最大的遗憾就是天一直阴阴的，下次还是得夏天来，享受地中海的盛夏～

That's about it.
We arrived in Barcelona on Monday evening and flew to Paris on Thursday afternoon.
The biggest regret of this trip was the weather — it stayed cloudy the whole time.
Next time, we'll have to come back in the summer and really enjoy the Mediterranean at its peak.
